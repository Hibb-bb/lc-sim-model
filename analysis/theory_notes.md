# Cross-survey joint-embedding SSL: the bottleneck argument and the proposed fix

Notes from the discussion, in the order the ideas came up.

## 1. Alignment = invariance

Joint-embedding losses (LeJEPA, BYOL, SimCLR's positive term) contain a term
that is zero iff `f(x_1) = f(x_2)` for positive pairs. So `f` is constant on
the equivalence classes generated by the positive-pair relation. With
augmentations `x_i = a_i(z)`, `a_i ∈ T`, that is `f ∘ a = f` for all `a ∈ T`:
invariance to a transformation set you chose. With two sensors `x_k = g_k(z)`
the relation is `t = g_2 ∘ g_1^{-1}`, so you are forced to be invariant to the
worse sensor's degradation whether you wanted it or not.

## 2. Why the worst source bounds everything (fixed-point argument)

At the exact optimum `h(z) := f(x_1) = f(x_2)`. Since `h(z) = f(x_2)` is a
function of `x_2` alone, the data-processing inequality gives

    I(f(x_1); z) ≤ I(x_2; z),   and symmetrically ≤ I(x_1; z),

and with K sources `I(f(x_k); z) ≤ min_j I(x_j; z)`. It is actually worse than
the min: `h` must be measurable w.r.t. every `σ(x_j)`, i.e. it lives in
`∩_j σ(x_j)`, the Gács–Körner common information, which is non-increasing in
K and often much smaller than any single `I(x_j; z)` (zero for two
observations with independent noise).

Linear toy: `z ~ N(0, I_d)`, `x_1 = z`, `x_2 = P z` with `P` a rank-k
projection, `f(x) = W x`. Zero alignment loss iff `W = W P`, so rank(W) ≤ k.
With several projections the row space of `W` must lie in `∩_j range(P_j)`,
whose dimension for generic subspaces is `max(0, k_1 + k_2 − d)`.

Soft version (alignment not exact, views independent given z):

    E‖f_1(x_1) − f_2(x_2)‖² = E_z Var(f_1|z) + E_z Var(f_2|z) + E_z‖E[f_1|z] − E[f_2|z]‖²

so each source's within-z variability is penalised separately and the
noisiest dominates.

## 3. Equivariance / predictors

The bottleneck comes from demanding `f(x_1) = f(x_2)`. An equivariant or
predictor-based objective demands `f(x_2) ≈ p_t(f(x_1))` instead, so the
lossy map is absorbed by `p_t` and `f(x_1)` may keep information `x_2` lacks.
Two consequences: if `g_2` is many-to-one no invertible group action works, you
need an asymmetric predictor; and direction matters, predicting the
low-quality embedding from the high-quality one is harmless, the reverse puts
the bottleneck back on the target side (`f(x_1) → E[f(x_1) | x_2]`). Symmetric
alignment imposes both directions at once.

A predictor on the low-quality side, however complex, cannot beat the bound:
at zero loss `f(x_1) = p(x_2)` is still a function of `x_2`.

## 4. The proposed design: split + quality-gated asymmetric alignment

Embedding `z = f(x) = [z_s | z_p]` (one vector, two named slices).

    L = Σ_pairs  w_{B→A} ‖p_{B→A}(z_s(x_B)) − sg[z_s(x_A)]‖²
               + w_{A→B} ‖p_{A→B}(z_s(x_A)) − sg[z_s(x_B)]‖²      (cross-survey, z_s only)
      + Σ_obs   E_{a,a'} ‖f(a(x)) − f(a'(x))‖²                    (augmentations, all of z)
      + λ SIGReg(f)                                                (isotropy, all of z)

with `w_{B→A} = σ((q_A − q_B)/τ)`, `w_{A→B} = 1 − w_{B→A}`, `q` a
per-observation quality score (e.g. log n_points − log median σ), `p` a small
predictor conditioned on the (source, target) band pair, `sg` stop-gradient.

Intended behaviour: `z_s` is bounded by the common information, `z_p` is
untouched by the cross-survey term and is shaped only by single-observation
augmentations; the best observation of a star is never pulled toward a worse
one; at equal quality the gate is 1/2 each way and the term reduces to a
symmetrised stop-gradient alignment. SIGReg's isotropy also decorrelates `z_s`
and `z_p`, so `z_p` cannot simply copy `z_s`. If a projector head is used, split
after it or use one projector per slice. Source labels are not needed for the
split (only the pairing); the gate needs per-observation quality, which for
light curves comes for free from the reported errors and cadence.

## 5. What the simulation found (see ../lcsim/RESULTS.md)

The fixed-point bound did not manifest in practice. With a bad partner
survey, LeJEPA and contrastive embeddings of the good survey kept as much or
more of the good-only feature than single-survey training; adding the bad
survey to a good+good pair *raised* the good survey's fine information; and
the split/gated variants were worse than plain LeJEPA, most likely because
their total alignment weight is twice LeJEPA's. Working hypothesis: in the
MSE + isotropy family the retention of low-variance private information is
governed by the alignment-to-regularizer ratio, not by the partner's quality,
because training sits far from the alignment optimum where the bound applies.
Contrastive learning kept the most private information, consistent with
instance discrimination rewarding any star-discriminative feature.
